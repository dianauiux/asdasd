### Post 1: Trust & proof (Fintech onboarding)

**Hooks**
1. ⭐ `Nobody quits a trading app because of the fees. They quit somewhere between "Verify your identity" and their first trade.`
2. `14 minutes. That's how long a new user waited before their first trade.`
3. `The most expensive screen in fintech is the one before the first win.`

```
Nobody quits a trading app because of the fees. They quit somewhere between "Verify your identity" and their first trade.

When I worked on the KYC and trading flows at Morpher, a new user needed about 14 minutes to reach a first trade. After the redesign it took about 6. First-session trade completion went from 52% to 71%.

Here's what I believe after that project:

In fintech, every minute before the first win is a minute for doubt. The user hasn't seen any value yet, but they've already handed over their passport. Trust is borrowed, and the clock is running.

So when I review an onboarding flow, I ask three things:
1. What is the very first win, and how many screens away is it?
2. Which steps are legally required, and which are just habit?
3. Where does the user wait without knowing why?

Most teams can answer the first question. Very few have measured the third.

What's the longest wait in your product's first session?

Save this for your next onboarding review.
```

### Post 2: Value + checklist (Web3)

**Hooks**
1. ⭐ `Web3 doesn't have a crypto problem. It has a "Connect wallet" problem.`
2. `About 4 in 10 users never got past connecting their wallet.`
3. `The scariest button in Web3 is two words long.`

```
Web3 doesn't have a crypto problem. It has a "Connect wallet" problem.

At Seascape, a Web3 gaming platform, about 4 in 10 wallet connections failed. I redesigned onboarding for players who had never touched crypto. Wallet-connection success went from about 58% to 81%, and first-game completion rose by about a third.

The players didn't need a lesson about blockchains. They needed to play.

When I audit a wallet flow now, this is my checklist:
- Can someone see the product before they connect anything?
- Does the screen say what happens next, in normal words?
- Is there a path for people with no wallet at all?
- If connection fails, does the user know what to do, or only that it failed?
- Do the top tasks take two clicks or four?

That last one is the easiest to measure and the easiest to ignore. On Seascape we took the main tasks from 4 clicks to 2.

Which Web3 product has the best onboarding you've seen?

Save the checklist for your next wallet flow.
```

### Post 3: Design x Business (Design systems, for founders)

**Hooks**
1. ⭐ `A design system isn't a Figma file. It's a decision about how fast you want to ship.`
2. `4 weeks to 2.5 weeks. Same product, same goals.`
3. `Every button your team redesigns from scratch is a small tax.`

```
A design system isn't a Figma file. It's a decision about how fast you want to ship.

At Asset Dash, after we built a shared web and mobile component library, design-to-dev handoff time dropped by about 30% and the shipping cycle went from 4 weeks to 2.5. At Morpher, a system of 60+ components cut the time to design a new feature by about 45%.

Most of that time comes from one thing: not making the same decision twice.

This is how I'd explain it to a founder. Every button your team redesigns from scratch is a small tax. On one screen it doesn't matter. Fifty screens and two platforms later, it's a whole sprint you'll never see on a roadmap.

You don't need a big system on day one. You need the 15 to 20 components your product uses every day, built properly, with the states your engineers keep asking about.

Where does your team lose the most time between design and code?
```
