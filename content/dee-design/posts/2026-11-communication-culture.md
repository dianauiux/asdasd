## Caption
```
We spend a lot of time teaching designers how to talk to clients.

Be clear. Be polite. Reply on time. Explain your decisions.

All true. But we rarely talk about the other side of the conversation.

Respect in a project goes both ways. And most clients aren't being difficult on purpose. Nobody ever showed them how a designer works.

So I don't wait for problems. I set the rules before the project starts:

→ My working hours and when to expect a reply
→ One channel for feedback, not five
→ What "urgent" means, and what can wait until morning
→ How many revision rounds we have, and how to give useful feedback
→ When I'm in deep work and won't be checking messages

It takes 15 minutes on the first call. It saves weeks of frustration on both sides.

A client who knows how you work trusts you faster. A designer who explains how they work gets treated like a partner, not a vendor.

Clear boundaries don't create distance. They create respect.

Save this for your next kickoff call.

Designers: do you set these rules on day one, or do you learn them the hard way?
Clients: what do you wish your designer had told you at the start?

#designclients #productdesign #uxdesign #freelancedesigner #designbusiness
```
