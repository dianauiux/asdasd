# Week 1 · People sign up, then never come back

### Mon 23.11 · How I solve it
```
When a founder tells me "people sign up and then disappear," I don't open Figma first.

I open the product and sign up as a new user. On my phone, in a hurry, like a real person.

Then I write down every moment I hesitate. Every field I don't understand. Every screen where I'm not sure what happens next.

Usually there are about five. One of them is where most people leave.

Then I look at the data to check I'm right. And only then do I start designing, and only that one step first.

It's not a glamorous process. But fixing one real step beats redesigning ten screens nobody was stuck on.

When was the last time you signed up for your own product?

#ProductDesign #UXDesign #SaaS #Onboarding
```

### Tue 24.11 · It's costing you
```
Every person who signs up and never comes back cost you money to get there.

The ad. The content. The sales call. The free trial.

Then they open the product, feel a little lost, and close the tab. They don't complain. They don't email you. They just quietly decide it isn't for them.

That's the expensive part. You paid for their attention and lost it in the first two minutes.

Most teams try to fix this with more emails. "Hey, we miss you!" But if the first experience was confusing, the email only reminds them of that.

The fix is almost always earlier. In the first screen, the first choice, the first moment they get something useful.

Founders: do you know what a new user sees in their first 60 seconds?

#Startup #SaaS #ProductDesign #Growth
```

# Week 2 · Visitors don't understand what the product does

### Mon 30.11 · How I solve it
```
When a landing page isn't converting, I start with a five-second test.

I show the page to someone for five seconds, close it, and ask: what does this company do?

If they can't answer, no button color will fix it.

Then I rewrite the headline with the founder. Not in marketing language, in the words they'd use to explain the product to a friend at dinner. That version is almost always better.

Only after that do I touch the layout.

Founders: could a stranger explain your product after five seconds on your homepage?

#Startup #LandingPage #UXDesign #ProductDesign
```

### Tue 01.12 · It's costing you
```
If people can't tell what your product does in five seconds, you're paying for traffic that leaves confused.

They don't email to ask. They don't scroll down to find out. They go back to Google and click the next result, which is often your competitor.

The worst part is you never see it. It shows up as "low conversion" in a report, and the team starts debating colors and buttons.

The fix is usually words, not design. One clear sentence at the top: who it's for, what it does, and why it's better.

What's the vaguest headline you've seen on a startup website? (No names needed.)

#Startup #Marketing #UXDesign #SaaS
```

# Week 3 · Users don't trust the product enough to pay or connect their data

### Mon 07.12 · How I solve it
```
When users don't trust a product, I look for the moment they're asked to give something.

Their card. Their bank account. Their company data. Their email, even.

Right before that moment, I ask: what do they know at this point? Do they know why we need it? What happens next? How to undo it?

Most trust problems aren't about the logo or the colors. They're about a request that comes too early, with too little explanation.

So I move the ask later, and I add one plain sentence next to it. That's often most of the work.

Where in your product do you ask users for the most?

#Fintech #AIProducts #UXDesign #Trust
```

### Tue 08.12 · It's costing you
```
A user who doesn't trust your product won't tell you. They'll just stop at the payment screen.

Or they'll sign up with a fake email. Or connect "just one test account." Or use the free plan forever.

In fintech and AI, this is the hidden cost nobody puts in a spreadsheet. People are interested enough to try, and not sure enough to commit.

Trust gets built in small places. A clear sentence about where data goes. A real human face. A way to undo things. A price that doesn't change at checkout.

What makes you trust a new app with your money or your data?

#Fintech #AI #ProductDesign #Trust
```

# Week 4 · Every new feature looks different, and shipping is slow

### Mon 14.12 · How I solve it
```
When a product starts to look like five different apps glued together, I don't start with a design system.

I start with screenshots. I take every screen of the product and put them side by side on one big canvas.

Then the team and I count. How many button styles? How many shades of gray? How many ways to show an error?

The count is usually funny, then a little painful. And then everyone agrees on what to fix first, without me having to convince anyone.

Only then do we build the components, starting with the ones used most.

How many button styles do you think your product has? Guess before you check.

#DesignSystems #ProductDesign #UXDesign #SaaS
```

### Tue 15.12 · It's costing you
```
A product without a design system costs you time every single sprint.

The designer redraws a button that already exists somewhere. The developer builds it again, slightly differently. QA finds that it doesn't match. Someone opens a ticket.

Multiply that by every feature, every week.

Users feel it too. When every screen looks a little different, the product feels unfinished, even if it works perfectly.

You don't need a 200-page system. You need the 10 components you use most, built once and used everywhere.

Developers: how much of your week goes to rebuilding things that already exist?

#DesignSystems #Engineering #ProductDesign #Startup
```
