# Week 1 · People sign up, then never come back

### Mon 23.11 · How I solve it · senior designer + mentor
```
When a founder tells me "people sign up and then disappear," the first thing I do surprises them.

I don't redesign anything. I sign up myself, on my phone, in a hurry, like a real user would.

Then I write down every moment I hesitate. Usually there are about five. One of them is where most people leave, and the data almost always confirms it.

The hard part isn't finding problems. It's choosing to fix one and leaving the other four for later. That's where seniority shows: saying "not yet" to things that are also broken.

I teach my students the same thing. Juniors want to fix everything they see. Good designers fix the thing that matters this month.

When was the last time you signed up for your own product?

#ProductDesign #UXDesign #SaaS #Onboarding
```

### Tue 24.11 · It's costing you · founder + senior designer
```
I run a business too, so I know this feeling.

You spend money to get someone's attention. Ads, content, calls, a free trial. They sign up. You feel good for a second.

Then they open the product, feel a little lost, and never come back. No complaint. No email. Just gone.

As a founder, that's the number that hurts most. Not the people who never found you. The people who found you, tried you, and quietly decided no.

Most teams answer with more emails. "We miss you!" But if the first five minutes were confusing, an email just reminds them of that.

The fix is earlier: the first screen, the first choice, the first small win.

Founders: do you know what a new user sees in their first 60 seconds?

#Startup #Founders #SaaS #ProductDesign

# Week 2 · Visitors don't understand what the product does

### Mon 30.11 · How I solve it · senior designer + mentor
```
When a landing page isn't converting, everyone wants to talk about design. I start with a five-second test.

I show the page to someone for five seconds, close it, and ask: what does this company do?

If they can't answer, no new layout will save it.

Then I ask the founder to explain the product to me like we're having dinner. That sentence, word for word, is usually a better headline than anything on the page.

When I review student work, I do the same. "Tell me what this is in one sentence." If they can't, we don't talk about colors yet.

Could a stranger explain your product after five seconds on your homepage?

#LandingPage #UXDesign #Startup #ProductDesign
```

```
Founder to founder: a confusing homepage is the most expensive page you own.

You pay to bring people there. They read the headline, don't get it, and go back to search. Often straight to your competitor, who explains the same thing more simply.

You never see it happen. It shows up as "low conversion" in a report, and then the team spends a week debating button colors.

From the design side, I can tell you: the fix is usually words, not pixels. Who it's for, what it does, why it's better. One sentence.

What's the vaguest headline you've seen on a startup website? (No names needed.)

# Week 3 · Users don't trust the product enough to pay or connect their data

### Mon 07.12 · How I solve it · senior designer + mentor
```
When users don't trust a product, I look for the moment they're asked to give something.

Their card. Their bank account. Their company data. Even their email.

Right before that moment, I ask three questions. Do they know why we need it? Do they know what happens next? Do they know how to undo it?

Most trust problems aren't about the logo. They're about a request that comes too early, with too little explanation.

I tell junior designers: trust isn't a visual style. You can't make something trustworthy with blue and a lock icon. You make it trustworthy by being clear at the moment it matters.

Where in your product do you ask users for the most?

#Fintech #AIProducts #UXDesign #Trust
```

### Tue 08.12 · It's costing you · founder + senior designer
```
As a founder, I know what "interested but not convinced" looks like on a dashboard.

People sign up with a fake email. Connect "just a test account." Stay on the free plan forever. Stop at the payment screen and never come back.

In fintech and AI this is the hidden cost. Curiosity is high, commitment is low, and nobody tells you why.

As a designer, I can tell you trust gets built in small places. One clear sentence about where the data goes. A way to undo things. A price that doesn't change at checkout.

None of that is expensive. Losing users who almost said yes is.

What makes you trust a new app with your money or your data?

#Fintech #AI #Founders #ProductDesign

# Week 4 · Every new feature looks different, and shipping is slow

### Mon 14.12 · How I solve it · senior designer + mentor
```
When a product starts to look like five apps glued together, I don't start by building a design system.

I start with screenshots. Every screen, side by side, on one big canvas. Then the team and I count. How many button styles? How many grays? How many ways to show an error?

The count is funny at first, then a little painful. And then everyone agrees on what to fix first, without me having to convince anyone.

That's something I wish more juniors knew: your job isn't to win the argument. It's to show the problem so clearly that there's no argument left.

How many button styles do you think your product has? Guess before you check.

#DesignSystems #ProductDesign #Mentorship #SaaS
```

### Tue 15.12 · It's costing you · founder + senior designer
```
When you run a company, you start to see design problems as time problems.

The designer redraws a button that already exists. The developer builds it again, slightly differently. QA spots the difference. Someone opens a ticket.

That's an hour here, two hours there, every sprint. As a founder, I'd rather spend those hours on things customers asked for.

Users feel it too. When every screen looks a little different, the product feels unfinished, even when it works.

You don't need a 200-page design system. You need your 10 most-used components built once and used everywhere.

Developers: how much of your week goes to rebuilding things that already exist?

#DesignSystems #Founders #Engineering #ProductDesign
```

### Fri 18.12 · One tip · mentor + senior designer
```
A 30-minute exercise I give students when their designs start to feel messy. It works on real products too:

Pick your 3 most common buttons. Screenshot every place they appear.

Choose one style for each, primary, secondary and text, and replace the rest.

The whole product will feel more finished, from three buttons. It's the fastest "senior" upgrade I know.

Save this for your next sprint.

Which component would you fix first in your product?
